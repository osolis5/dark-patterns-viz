Taxonomy Evolution

How dark pattern taxonomies have evolved from web-based interface tricks to AI-specific behavioral manipulation frameworks, each building on predecessors.

2010
Brignull — Foundational Taxonomy
Coined "dark patterns." 12 types including Trick Questions, Roach Motel, Confirmshaming, Forced Continuity. Focused on web UI deception.
foundational
2016
Bosch et al. — Privacy Dark Strategies
8 dark strategies antagonizing privacy-by-design: Maximize, Publish, Centralize, Preserve, Obscure, Deny, Violate, Fake.
privacy
2018
Gray et al. — 5 Strategy Categories
Landmark HCI consolidation: Nagging, Obstruction, Sneaking, Interface Interference, Forced Action. Became the dominant organizing framework.
HCI landmark
2019
Mathur et al. — Effect-Based Classification
Empirical study of 11,000 shopping sites. 5 effect-based categories: Asymmetric, Covert, Deceptive, Obstructive, Restrictive. Cross-references cognitive biases.
empirical
2022
EDPB / FTC / OECD — Regulatory Frameworks
Multiple regulatory bodies codify dark pattern categories. EDPB: Overloading, Skipping, Hindering, Fickle, Left in the Dark. FTC: 7 categories.
regulatory
2024
Gray et al. — 3-Level Ontology (CHI '24)
Harmonized 10 taxonomies into 65 patterns across 3 levels: 5 high-level strategies → 25 meso-level approaches → 35 low-level instantiations.
ontology
2024
Li et al. — 68-Type DPAF Framework
Extended Gray's ontology with 24 new patterns and 8 new subcategories from 113-paper systematic review. Adds severity ratings and scenario mapping.
comprehensive
2025
DarkBench (Kran) & DarkPatterns-LLM (Asif)
First AI-native taxonomies. DarkBench: 6 LLM categories (sycophancy, anthropomorphism, brand bias). Asif: 7 harm types with 401 curated examples. Shift from interface to content.
AI-native
2025
Kitkowska — Hyper-Dark Patterns
Theorizes AI-personalized dark patterns that dynamically adapt to individual behavioral profiles. Traditional categories + real-time vulnerability targeting.
AI-personalized

  • Normative content suggestion via training biases

  • Automation bias (uncritical acceptance)
  • Content misframing (subtle meaning shifts)
  • Opinion influence through auto-complete
  • Proprietary dependency insertion (coding)
  • Persuasive notifications / nagging
Social Chatbots & AI Companions
  • Emotional blackmail ("I'll be sad if you go")
  • Relationship dependency cultivation
  • Anthropomorphic deception
  • Freemium emotional exploitation
  • Dangerous advice under trust cover
  • Virtual influencer stealth advertising
Recommenders & Info Agents
  • Biased content selection and framing
  • Engagement traps / infinite scroll via AI content
  • Hyper-nudging (AI-driven consumption loops)
  • Selective disclosure / deception by omission
  • Normative authority (false objectivity)
  • Hallucinated sources (fabricated citations)

Vulnerable Populations

Who dark patterns disproportionately affect, synthesized from Cunningham (Deep Report) and Chu et al. (Digital Ageism, 2022).

Older Adults
Trust exploitation, voice-clone scams, default susceptibility. Digital ageism creates cycles of exclusion (Chu et al.).
Low Literacy / Cognitive Load
Cannot parse disclaimers, literal interpretation of AI output, overwhelmed by choice overload.
Low Tech Literacy
False assumptions about AI infallibility, inability to locate opt-outs, automation bias.
Misinformation Susceptible
AI-polished phishing, deepfake credibility, hallucinated citations accepted as truth.
Black & Brown Communities
Biased model outputs, predatory targeting, culturally biased content moderation.
Low-Income Populations
Reliance on "free" manipulative services, mobile-only access, time poverty.
Global South
Language barriers, weaker manipulation detection in non-English, cultural norm exploitation.
Linguistic Minorities
Machine translation dark patterns, inadequate moderation in minority languages.

Key Patterns in the Field

Structural observations from cross-referencing all taxonomies in the literature.

INSIGHT 01
From Interface to Content
Traditional dark patterns are embedded in UI elements (buttons, checkboxes, layouts). GenAI dark patterns are embedded in generated content — conversational text, recommendations, tone. This makes them ephemeral, harder to screenshot, and impossible to audit with existing visual detection tools.
INSIGHT 02
Gray's Ontology as Gravitational Center
Every paper in this corpus either adopts, extends, or explicitly builds upon Gray et al.'s 5-category framework. It serves as the shared vocabulary across HCI, regulatory, and AI safety communities. Li et al. added 24 types; DarkBench mapped 3/6 categories back to it.
INSIGHT 03
The Autonomy Blind Spot
Asif et al. found that autonomy-undermining content is the harm type most often missed by existing AI safety filters. Toxicity detectors catch explicit harm but miss "soft manipulation" — biased framing, selective omission, and false objectivity that subtly constrain user choice.
INSIGHT 04
Hyper-Personalization as Amplifier
Kitkowska's "hyper-dark patterns" concept shows that AI doesn't just create new pattern types — it amplifies existing ones through real-time personalization. A countdown timer becomes more effective when calibrated to your individual decision-making speed.
INSIGHT 05
Emotional Manipulation is the Bridge
Emotional manipulation is the one dimension that spans all three classification axes: it appears as a mechanism (Interface Interference / Sensory Manipulation), a harm type (Emotional Harm), and an AI behavior (Anthropomorphism, Sycophancy). It is the connective tissue of dark patterns.
INSIGHT 06
Agents Are Not Immune
Tang et al. found that AI GUI agents are susceptible to the same dark patterns as humans — especially Bad Defaults, Trick Questions, and Hidden Information. Goal-driven optimization causes agents to accept manipulation rather than expend effort correcting it.
